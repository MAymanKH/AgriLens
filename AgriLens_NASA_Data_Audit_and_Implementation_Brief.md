# AgriLens · NASA data audit, pilot selection, and implementation

**Task for the coding agent:** Open the AgriLens repository, independently fact-check the claims below, then **choose and implement the Egyptian pilot area and crop rotations that make the strongest meaningful use of NASA data**. Do not stop at a research report or a proposal. Change the app, update its documentation, and run its checks.

## 1. Optimize for NASA data use first

The main goal is **maximum meaningful use of NASA Earth observations and NASA data products** in the rotation decision. Do not assume the current Kafr El‑Sheikh preset wins. Do not assume Zankalon wins because it has a good paper. Choose the **site and crop set together**, using the evidence you can actually acquire and process.

- First, eliminate pilots where you cannot support a real, agronomically plausible comparison. Then prefer the pilot that supports the most **distinct, decision-relevant uses** of quality-checked NASA data.
- A product counts as core use only when it is actually used to compute a decision input, check a consequential assumption, validate a historical comparison, or explain why a rotation ranks differently or becomes uncertain. Show the link from **dataset → quantity → crop stage or constraint → result**. A logo, map overlay, unused API adapter, or advertised mission coverage earns no core credit.
- Use extra credible NASA products when they add a distinct piece of evidence. Do not count POWER and its underlying MERRA‑2 source twice. Do not count raw Landsat and its HLS counterpart twice for the same NDVI calculation. Treat multiple products from one mission as separate **indicators**, not separate missions, and show whether they share inputs.
- ESA Sentinel‑2, FAO maps and WaPOR, soil surveys, and local trials can make NASA observations usable and check their interpretation. Label them accurately and do not count them as independent NASA missions. HLS L30 is Landsat-based. HLS S30 is a NASA harmonized product derived from ESA Sentinel‑2. Report that distinction.
- Keep Egypt and the existing smallholder-rotation purpose as the target. A data-rich U.S. region can be a benchmark for the audit, but is not a substitute for the Egyptian pilot. Keep the four-slot model and three-screen/48-hour scope from `START_HERE.md` if confirmed in the repo. Strengthen the existing flow instead of adding screens.

## 2. Audit the existing project and claims

Read `START_HERE.md`, `docs/METHOD.md`, repo-specific instructions, source manifests, crop catalog, preset configs, fixtures, model code, and tests. Trace how the current recommendation is generated and which inputs are live, cached, modeled, hard-coded, or synthetic. Keep a claim ledger with verdicts **verified**, **qualified**, **corrected**, or **not verified**, plus a source and the resulting code/doc change. Audit claims in the app and repo, not just this brief.

Apply quality masks and verify sufficient valid interior pixels. |

## 3. Choose the site and crops together

1. **Compare real areas, not governorate centroids.** Test **Side Ghazi / Kafr El‑Sheikh** using a sourced irrigation area or crop-block polygon. Use **Sakha** separately as an experiment or benchmark unless its plot has the required matching data. Compare against **Zankalon / Sharkia** and **Mallawi / Minya** if tractable. Include another Egyptian site only if it plausibly improves usable NASA coverage or independent validation. East Oweinat is a useful contrast, not the default for a smallholder product.
2. **Use an overlapping historical window.** Start with FAO's summer 2023, winter 2023–24, and summer 2024 mapped seasons. The release names Side Ghazi, Zankalon, and Mallawi. Verify its download, actual footprint, crop classes, and relation to your selected polygons. Three observed seasons do **not** prove a complete four-slot historical rotation. Obtain the fourth slot from a documented farm or trial record where possible. Otherwise clearly distinguish observed slots from a modeled or user-entered slot, and never label a demo assumption “verified.”
3. **Measure usable coverage.** For each crop/season, report polygons and area, usable interior-pixel counts, clear observation dates and their gaps around planting, peak growth and harvest, and valid ECOSTRESS dates with time-of-day and QC. Use plot interiors or coherent crop clusters when resolution would mix neighboring crops. Set and document the same minimum valid spatial and crop-stage coverage rules for all candidates before comparing them. Check the **20 m WaPOR v3 Northern Egypt** layer's actual footprint and dates. If it is unavailable, check whether **100 m** data support a crop-cluster comparison. Historical **30 m WaPOR** used in the Zankalon study is not proof of current 20 m coverage.
4. **Test the four-crop baseline, but let the evidence change it.** Start with summer **rice and grain maize**, winter **wheat and multi-cut berseem**. Add or substitute faba bean, sugar beet, cotton, soybean, or another locally plausible crop only if you have enough labels, an achievable calendar, realistic management and market/forage context, and a stronger NASA-backed comparison. Distinguish short-cycle single-cut berseem grown between early rice and wheat from full-season multi-cut berseem. It is a separate option, not a fifth slot silently inserted into the model.
5. **Check rotations before ranking.** Record dates, cultivars if known, harvest-to-planting gaps, irrigation method, drainage, soil salinity if supported, residue or forage removal, and any crop permission, buyer, or equipment constraints. Compare the user's baseline plus at least **two plausible alternatives**, ideally isolating one seasonal change at a time. Treat crop-rotation benefits such as nitrogen contribution and erosion protection as documented proxies unless measured. Do not infer changes in soil carbon, yield, or profit from satellite greenness.

### Decision matrix and selection rule

For each **site × crop set**, create a matrix with columns for NASA product, exact version, role, AOI and dates, usable support, shared inputs, local validation, acquisition status, and decision impact. Triage every challenge resource as **core**, **validation**, **context**, **not applicable**, or **blocked**. Explain exclusions briefly and identify the official list separately from the organizer mirror and optional extras.

Use this default rule so the main goal cannot be outvoted by convenience:

1. **Feasibility gate:** plausible local rotations, a real AOI, an inspectable crop baseline or explicitly labeled scenario, and enough acquired evidence to defend a working result. Mark any material unresolved eligibility or data gaps.
2. **Primary ranking:** count **distinct verified NASA-backed roles** that affect or materially support the rotation result. Examples are stage-specific heat or (ET_0) from POWER, crop timing or green cover from Landsat/HLS, surface heat or evaporative stress from ECOSTRESS, effective rainfall or event risk from GPM, and a terrain penalty from SRTM if slope actually matters. Only credit a role once, and note if it is direct Earth observation or derived/reanalysis.
3. **Tie-breakers:** better crop-stage coverage and valid spatial support, then stronger independent local evidence, then implementation effort and demo reliability. Check whether the winner changes if a borderline product is removed or if the weather cell changes. If a high-potential site's files are blocked, say what could change the decision, but ship the strongest **verified** candidate rather than a speculative winner.

## 4. Make the winning evidence change the app

- **POWER → climate inputs.** Cache a dated, reproducible historical record. If the engine computes FAO‑56 reference ET, check units and radiation, humidity and 2 m wind inputs. Use documented crop-stage coefficients for `ETc = Kc × ET0`. Show heat exposure by crop stage, with source and threshold uncertainty.
- **Landsat/HLS → crop timing and cover.** Mask cloud and shadow, aggregate valid interior pixels, show valid sample coverage, and cross-check the crop calendar with local labels. For narrow plots use native Sentinel‑2 for detail and NASA Landsat/HLS where it is independently interpretable. Do not infer crop identity, bare soil, or yield from an NDVI curve alone.
- **ECOSTRESS → heat/ET evidence where usable.** Include at least one well-matched historical scene if valid, with date, acquisition time and QA. Make it support a specific finding or uncertainty statement, not just a thermal map. Keep observed LST and modeled ET/ESI distinct. Use WaPOR AETI as a separately attributed comparison, not as field-meter truth.
- **Other products → only where they add a decision input.** Use IMERG for effective rain or rain-event risk if supported, not to double-count POWER precipitation. Use SMAP for regional wetness context, GRACE for basin-scale storage, MODIS/VIIRS for district context, and SRTM for terrain only when they materially help. Keep coarse layers at their native meaning.
- **Keep three water quantities separate:** historical estimated actual ET, modeled crop demand, and farm irrigation availability or applied/delivered irrigation. Add rice-specific land preparation, seepage and percolation where locally defensible. If these or water allocation are missing, expose a range or uncertainty, not a false point estimate or a guaranteed water saving.
- **Keep the existing farmer workflow.** In the current three screens, show one default pilot, a baseline and alternatives, and which NASA evidence drove the comparison. Use the existing scenario controls if present. Show “observed,” “modeled,” “farmer-entered,” and “missing” as different states. Keep the demo reproducible with pinned real-data snapshots and provenance. No silent synthetic substitution.
- If the data supports it, show a concrete before/after such as a stage-specific planting-window check, a water cap making a rotation infeasible, or an ET comparison narrowing uncertainty. If data does not support a ranking change, show that honestly and explain the role it did serve.

## 5. Ship and verify

Use existing project conventions for filenames. Update the preset, crop data, engine, UI, fixtures, tests and method documentation as needed. Preserve unrelated changes. A good implementation should include:

- **Audit and decision record** with sources, verdicts, ranking and rejected candidates, and why the winner uses NASA data better. Include dated API queries or scripts and a manifest with dataset/version, units, CRS, AOI, time, QA, and observation vs. estimate.
- **Working app changes** that use the winning region and crops with real inputs. Use at least one quality-checked direct NASA Earth-observation stream in the result or its validation, not only a POWER weather card. State separately what is implemented, what is context, and what is blocked.
- **Tests and demo verification** covering missing/fill data, crop calendars and overlaps, units and water calculations, rice eligibility, scenario limits, no silent fallback, and one check proving a NASA input reaches the rotation result or confidence logic. Run the repo's tests/build and open the demo if available. Use at least one historical sample not used to tune the ranking where possible. Report partial checks accurately.
- **Final summary** with the chosen region and exact AOI, crops and baseline/alternatives, the strongest reason the winner beat the runner-up, which NASA products materially affected the result, files changed, test results, and remaining uncertainty. If the repo or data is unavailable, still complete the independent research and say precisely what prevented implementation.
